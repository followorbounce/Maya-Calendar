Haab' · 18 × 20 + 5

The 365-day solar calendar

Eighteen named months of twenty days, then Wayeb' — five "nameless" days that close the year. It is called a vague year because it has no leap-day correction: 365 whole days against a tropical year of about 365.2422, so the Haab' slips roughly one day every four years against the seasons and completes a full lap in about 1,507 years.

A glyphic quirk

"Seating," not day zero

Maya scribes did not write "0 Pop." The first day of a month is its seating — chum — as though the month takes its seat before its count begins. So a date runs: seating of Pop, then 1 Pop, 2 Pop … up to 19 Pop, then the seating of the next month. This reference writes the seating day as "Seating of <month>."

The same logic governs the Long Count's period endings, which is why a k'atun ends on a day like 13.0.0.0.0 — a completion, a seating of the next unit.

Year bearers

The four days that can carry a year

Because 365 mod 20 = 5, only four of the twenty day signs can ever coincide with a fixed Haab' station — and the set depends on which station is used as the New Year anchor. Two systems are historically attested.
